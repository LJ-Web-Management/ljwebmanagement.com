## How the platforms differ

| Platform | Best for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Zapier** | Non-technical teams, simple workflows, the widest choice of app integrations | Per-task pricing that grows with volume |
| **Make** | Visual, multi-step workflows with branching and data mapping | A steeper learning curve than Zapier |
| **n8n** | Technical teams, high volume, self-hosting and data control | Needs someone to host, update, and maintain it |
| **Power Automate** | Businesses built on Microsoft 365 | Licensing can be confusing; fewer non-Microsoft integrations |
| **Custom or done-for-you** | Complex, sensitive, or business-critical workflows | Higher upfront cost; choose a partner who documents their work |

Pricing and features change often, so check each vendor's site before you commit. The full [Zapier vs. Make vs. n8n comparison](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/zapier-vs-make-vs-n8n/) goes deeper on how each one works and charges.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which is better, Zapier or Make?**

Zapier is easier to learn and connects to more apps out of the box. Make offers more control over branching and data, and is usually more economical at higher volumes. Non-technical teams often start with Zapier; tech-comfortable builders often prefer Make.

**Is n8n free?**

n8n is source-available and can be self-hosted, which avoids per-run fees but means paying for and maintaining a server. It also offers a paid cloud version. Check the current license and pricing on n8n's site.

**Can I switch platforms later?**

Yes, though workflows must be rebuilt, since platforms do not share a format. Documenting each workflow's trigger, steps, and data makes switching far easier.
