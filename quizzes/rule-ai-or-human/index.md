## Who should do the work?

The best automation plans give each task to the right worker: fixed rules for predictable steps, AI for language and judgment within limits, and people for decisions that need authority, empathy, or accountability.

## A simple way to decide

- **Rule-based automation** for predictable steps with structured data: reminders, notifications, moving records between apps, creating tasks.
- **An AI step** where the input is language or varies too much for rules: sorting messages, extracting data from documents, summarizing, drafting.
- **A person** for decisions that need authority, empathy, negotiation, or accountability, and for reviewing AI output where mistakes are costly.

Most real workflows mix all three. A support workflow might use a rule to log every ticket, AI to sort and draft replies, and a person to approve refunds. The guide to [AI vs. automation](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/ai-vs-automation/) explains how to combine them.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I use AI instead of regular automation?**

Use AI when a step requires understanding or producing language, or when inputs vary too much for fixed rules, such as free-text emails or documents in many layouts. Use regular automation for everything predictable; it is cheaper, faster, and easier to audit.

**Which tasks should never be automated?**

Decisions with legal, financial, or personal consequences that need accountability, such as hiring decisions, sensitive HR conversations, and binding commitments, should stay with people. Automation can still gather information and schedule the work around them.

**What does human in the loop mean?**

It means the workflow pauses for a person to review or approve a step before it continues, such as approving an AI-drafted email before it is sent.
