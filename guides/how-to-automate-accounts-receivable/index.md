# How to Automate Accounts Receivable and Get Paid Faster

> A practical playbook for automating accounts receivable: same-day invoicing, online payments, reminder schedules, cash application, recurring billing, and…

A practical playbook for automating accounts receivable: same-day invoicing, online payments, reminder schedules, cash application, recurring billing, and the metrics that show it is working.

LJ Web ManagementAutomation team Updated October 25, 2026 4 min read

Key takeaways

- The clock on getting paid starts when the invoice is sent, so send it the moment work is done.
- Every invoice should include a one-click way to pay by card or ACH.
- A consistent, courteous reminder schedule collects more than occasional manual chasing.
- Automated cash application and an aging dashboard let staff focus only on accounts that truly need a call.

Many businesses that feel short on cash are not short on revenue. They are short on collected revenue. Invoices go out days after the work, customers lose them in an inbox, reminders depend on someone remembering, and payments get applied to the wrong account. Each delay is small; together they tie up weeks of cash.

Accounts receivable automation closes those gaps. This playbook covers each step from invoice to cash and how to automate it.

## Step 1: Invoice the same day the work is done

Payment terms start counting when the customer receives the invoice. If invoices go out weekly or monthly in a batch, you have added days or weeks to every payment. Instead, trigger invoices from the event that completes the work:

- A job marked complete in your [field service system](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/solutions/operations-field-service/field-service/).
- An order marked shipped in your store or ERP.
- A milestone approved in your project tool.
- Approved timesheets at the end of each billing period.
- A subscription renewal date for [recurring billing](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/solutions/finance-billing/membership-billing/).

## Step 2: Make paying effortless

Every invoice should carry a secure payment link that accepts card and ACH, and customers should be able to save a payment method or enroll in autopay. Text-to-pay links work especially well for consumers and small businesses. Larger B2B invoices are often cheaper to collect by ACH, so make it the easy default. See [online payments and checkout integration](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/solutions/finance-billing/online-payments-checkout/).

## Step 3: Run a consistent reminder schedule

A schedule that runs automatically for every invoice collects more than sporadic manual follow-up. A typical sequence:

| Timing | Message |
| --- | --- |
| On send | Invoice with payment link and a brief thank-you |
| 3 days before due | Friendly heads-up with the payment link |
| On the due date | Reminder that payment is due today |
| 7 days overdue | Polite overdue notice, offer to answer questions |
| 14 to 21 days overdue | Firmer notice, mention late fees if your terms include them |
| 30+ days overdue | Task created for a person to call; statement sent |

**Stop on payment and on dispute**

Nothing damages a relationship faster than a reminder for a bill that was already paid or is under discussion. Reminders must stop the moment payment posts or someone marks the invoice as disputed.

## Step 4: Automate cash application

When payments arrive by card, ACH, check, or bank transfer, match them to the right invoices automatically using invoice numbers, amounts, and customer details. Payments processed through your invoicing tool apply themselves; bank deposits can be matched with rules and AI, with exceptions routed to a person. This keeps customer balances accurate, which keeps reminders accurate.

## Step 5: Watch the aging report, not the inbox

With invoicing, reminders, and cash application automated, your team's job becomes exception handling. An AR aging dashboard grouped by customer and days overdue shows exactly which accounts need a personal call. Add alerts for large invoices crossing 30 days and for customers whose payment pattern is slipping.

## Step 6: Connect AR to the rest of finance

AR automation delivers the most when it is part of a connected finance stack:

- Invoices and payments sync to QuickBooks, Xero, or your ERP without re-keying.
- Processor payouts are reconciled with fees and refunds automatically.
- The [month-end close](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/solutions/finance-billing/month-end-close/) starts with reconciled receivables instead of a cleanup project.
- Payables are handled with the same discipline through [AP automation](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/solutions/finance-billing/accounts-payable-expense/).

## Metrics that show it is working

- Days from work completed to invoice sent (target: same day).
- Days sales outstanding (DSO).
- Percentage of invoices paid by the due date.
- Percentage of payments made online or by autopay.
- Balance over 60 days as a share of total receivables.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is accounts receivable automation?**

Accounts receivable automation uses software to create and send invoices, collect payments online, send payment reminders, match payments to invoices, and report on outstanding balances with minimal manual work.

**What is DSO?**

Days sales outstanding (DSO) measures the average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale. Lower DSO means faster cash flow. Automating invoicing and reminders is one of the most direct ways to reduce it.

**Will automated payment reminders annoy customers?**

Courteous, accurate reminders are generally appreciated because they prevent surprises. Reminders should stop automatically when payment arrives or when a customer raises a dispute.

**Which tools are used for AR automation?**

Common options include QuickBooks and Xero built-in tools, Stripe and Square invoicing, Bill.com, and dedicated AR platforms, often connected to a CRM, field service, or ERP system.
