# How to Automate Review Requests Without Annoying Customers

> Get more reviews by asking every customer at the right moment, with the right link, while staying inside Google's review policies. Timing, templates, and…

Get more reviews by asking every customer at the right moment, with the right link, while staying inside Google's review policies. Timing, templates, and setup.

LJ Web ManagementAutomation team Updated October 25, 2026 4 min read

Key takeaways

- The best time to ask is right after a good experience, not days later in a batch.
- Ask every customer the same way. Asking only the happy ones (review gating) breaks Google's policies.
- Send a direct link to your review form so leaving a review takes seconds.
- Alert a manager immediately when a customer reports a problem, and respond to every review.

Reviews are one of the strongest signals in local search and one of the first things a new customer checks. Most businesses know this and still have far fewer reviews than happy customers. The reason is not that people refuse; it is that nobody asked at the right moment, or asking took too much effort.

Automating review requests solves the consistency problem. Every customer gets asked, at the right time, with a link that takes them straight to the review form.

## Start with the rules

Before building anything, know the guardrails. Google's review policy prohibits **selectively soliciting positive reviews** and **discouraging negative ones**. In practice, that means you should not send a "How did we do?" survey and then only show the review link to people who answered five stars. The FTC has also taken action against review practices that mislead consumers, including suppressing negative reviews and paying for positive ones.

**The compliant approach**

Ask every customer for an honest review using the same message. Separately, make it easy for anyone to tell you about a problem directly, and alert a manager immediately when they do. Fixing the issue is the best reputation strategy there is.

## Timing is everything

Trigger the request from the event that marks a finished, successful experience:

| Business type | Trigger | Suggested delay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home services | Job marked complete and paid | 1 to 3 hours |
| Clinics and salons | Appointment checked out | 2 to 4 hours |
| Restaurants | Reservation completed or order closed | Next morning |
| E-commerce | Order delivered | 3 to 7 days |
| Real estate and legal | Closing or case resolution | 1 to 3 days |

Avoid sending review requests to anyone with an open complaint, an unresolved support ticket, or a disputed invoice. That is not gating; it is good manners, and it should apply equally to everyone in that situation.

## Make it a one-tap action

The biggest drop-off happens between "sure, I'll leave a review" and actually finding where to do it. Use a direct link to your Google review form (available from your Google Business Profile as "Ask for reviews" or "Share review form") rather than a link to your search listing. On mobile, a text with that link is usually the fastest path.

## Templates

**Text after a service:**

> Hi Chris, thanks for choosing Ridge Roofing today! If you have a minute, we'd really appreciate an honest review. It helps neighbors find us: \[review link\]. Any issues with the work? Just reply here and Sam will make it right.

**Gentle reminder (3 to 5 days later, if no review detected):**

> Hi Chris, one quick favor: if you have 30 seconds, an honest review of your roof repair would mean a lot to our small team. \[review link\] Thanks either way!

Notice that the "any issues?" line is offered to everyone, and it does not replace the review link. That keeps the process fair while still giving unhappy customers a direct line to you.

## Close the loop on every review

Requesting reviews is half the system. The other half is responding:

- **New review alerts** sent to the owner or manager, with the rating and text.
- **Response reminders** if a review has not been answered within a day or two.
- **Escalation** for low ratings, so someone reaches out personally and quickly.
- **Weekly digest** of new reviews across Google, Facebook, Yelp, and industry sites.

Thoughtful public responses, especially to critical reviews, show future customers how you handle problems. AI can draft a first response for a manager to edit, which keeps response times fast without sounding canned.

## Turn reviews into marketing

With permission, great reviews can feed testimonials on your website, social posts, and proposals. Some businesses also follow a positive review with a referral request a few weeks later, when the relationship is warm.

## What to track

- Review requests sent vs. reviews received
- Average rating over time
- Median time to respond to new reviews
- Reviews per location or per technician

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the best time to ask for a review?**

Shortly after the customer experiences the result: when the job is finished, the order is delivered, or the appointment ends. For services, a request within a few hours of completion usually works well. For products, wait until the item has arrived and had a day or two of use.

**What is review gating and why should I avoid it?**

Review gating means asking customers how they feel first and only sending the satisfied ones to a public review site. Google's policies prohibit selectively soliciting positive reviews or discouraging negative ones, and the FTC also targets review practices that mislead consumers. Ask everyone, and handle complaints by fixing them, not by hiding them.

**How many review reminders should I send?**

One request and one gentle reminder a few days later is plenty. More than that starts to feel pushy. Stop the sequence as soon as a review is detected if your tools support it.

**Can I offer incentives for reviews?**

Paying for or incentivizing reviews conflicts with the policies of Google and most review platforms, and can violate FTC rules on consumer reviews. Make reviewing easy and well timed instead.
