    Glossary

    Automation and AI glossary

    Plain-English definitions of the business automation and AI terms you will run into, from API and webhook to AI agent, RPA, and large language model. Each term links to a guide or lesson that goes deeper.

    68 terms
    ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

    A

    Accounts payable automation
    Software that captures supplier invoices, extracts their details, matches them to purchase orders, routes them for approval, and schedules payment, so the bills you pay need far less manual data entry. How to automate invoice processing
    Agentic workflow
    A workflow in which an AI agent plans and carries out some of the steps itself, usually inside limits set by a larger rule-based process and with human approval for risky actions. What is an AI agent?
    AI agent
    Software built around an AI model that is given a goal and a set of tools (such as a calendar, CRM, or email) and decides which steps to take to reach that goal, checking the results as it goes. What is an AI agent?
    API Application Programming Interface
    A defined way for one piece of software to request data or actions from another. Most modern business apps offer an API, which is what lets automation tools connect them. Lesson: connecting your tools
    API key
    A secret code that identifies your account when software calls an API. Treat it like a password: store it securely, give each integration its own key, and never paste it anywhere public.
    Approval workflow
    An automation that routes a request (an expense, a discount, a document) to the right person to approve or reject, then continues based on their decision. Document workflow automation
    Artificial intelligence (AI)
    Software that performs tasks that normally need human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing images, or making predictions. In business automation, AI most often reads, sorts, extracts, summarizes, and drafts text. AI vs. automation
    Automation platform
    Software for building workflows that connect apps with little or no code, such as Zapier, Make, n8n, or Microsoft Power Automate. Also called an integration platform or iPaaS. Zapier vs. Make vs. n8n

    B

    Batch processing
    Handling records in groups on a schedule (for example, every night) instead of one at a time as they arrive. Common for large data loads, reports, and discounted AI jobs that are not urgent.
    Business process automation (BPA)
    Using software to run multi-step business processes, such as onboarding, invoicing, or approvals, with little manual work. BPA usually spans several apps, people, and decisions. Business process automation guide
    Business process management (BPM)
    A discipline for designing, measuring, and improving how work gets done. Automation is one tool BPM uses; BPM also covers documentation, ownership, and continuous improvement. Business process automation guide

    C

    Chatbot
    Software that holds a text conversation with people on a website, in a messaging app, or inside a product. Modern chatbots use AI language models; older ones follow scripted decision trees. How to automate customer support
    Condition
    A rule inside a workflow that checks something (Is the order over $500? Did the lead reply?) and decides which steps run next. Also called a filter, branch, path, or router. Lesson: triggers, actions, and conditions
    Context window
    The maximum amount of text an AI model can consider at once, including your instructions, any documents you provide, and its reply. It is measured in tokens. Lesson: how AI language models work
    Copilot
    An AI assistant built into an app that helps a person do their work, such as drafting, summarizing, or suggesting next steps, while the person stays in control of the result.
    CRM Customer Relationship Management
    Software that keeps customer and prospect records, conversations, and deals in one place. A CRM is often the hub that business automations read from and write to. CRM automation guide

    D

    Data entry automation
    Capturing information from forms, emails, documents, or other apps and entering it into your systems automatically instead of retyping it. How to automate data entry
    Data mapping
    Matching fields in one app to fields in another (for example, a form's Phone field to the CRM's Mobile field) so data lands in the right place when an automation moves it.
    Deduplication
    Finding and merging duplicate records, such as the same customer entered twice, so automations do not send double messages or build reports on conflicting data.
    Document workflow automation
    Automating how documents are requested, collected, generated, reviewed, approved, signed, and filed. Document workflow automation guide
    Drip campaign
    A series of automated messages sent on a schedule or in response to what a person does, such as a welcome sequence or a lead nurture series. How to automate emails

    E

    Embedding
    A list of numbers that represents the meaning of a piece of text, letting software find content with similar meaning even when the wording differs. Embeddings power semantic search and retrieval-augmented generation.
    ERP Enterprise Resource Planning
    Software that runs core operations such as inventory, purchasing, accounting, and fulfillment in one connected system.
    Error handling
    How an automation responds when a step fails, such as retrying, skipping the record, logging the problem, or alerting a person to step in. Lesson: testing, monitoring, and handling errors
    ETL Extract, Transform, Load
    A process that pulls data out of source systems, cleans and reshapes it, and loads it into a destination such as a reporting database or spreadsheet. How to automate reports

    F

    Fine-tuning
    Further training an existing AI model on your own examples so it adopts a specific style or task. Most small business needs are better served by good prompts and retrieval than by fine-tuning.

    G

    Generative AI
    AI that creates new content, such as text, images, audio, or code, in response to a prompt. Large language models are the type most used in business automation. AI vs. automation
    Guardrails
    Limits placed on an AI system to keep it safe and on task, such as restricting which tools it may use, what it may discuss, and which actions need human approval. Lesson: AI agents and guardrails

    H

    Hallucination
    When an AI model produces information that sounds confident and plausible but is false. Grounding the model in your own data and reviewing its output reduces the risk. Lesson: data, privacy, and accuracy
    Human in the loop
    A workflow design in which a person reviews or approves certain steps, such as an AI-drafted reply or a large refund, before the automation continues. AI vs. automation

    I

    Integration
    A connection that lets two apps share data or trigger actions in each other. It may be built into the apps (native), created with an automation platform, or written in code. Lesson: connecting your tools
    Intelligent document processing (IDP)
    Using OCR and AI together to classify documents and extract their data, such as reading invoices, forms, receipts, and contracts. How to automate invoice processing
    iPaaS Integration Platform as a Service
    Cloud software for connecting apps and automating the workflows between them. Zapier, Make, and Microsoft Power Automate are common examples.

    J

    JSON
    A simple text format for structured data, written as labeled fields inside curly braces. Most APIs and webhooks send data as JSON, and AI steps can be asked to reply in it so a workflow can read the answer.

    L

    Large language model (LLM)
    An AI model trained on large amounts of text to understand and generate language. LLMs power chat assistants and the AI steps in modern automations. Lesson: how AI language models work
    Lead nurturing
    Staying in touch with prospects who are not ready to buy yet, through helpful automated messages over time, so you are the obvious choice when they are. How to automate lead follow-up
    Lead routing
    Automatically assigning each new lead to the right salesperson or team based on rules such as location, service, or deal size. How to automate lead follow-up
    Low-code
    Building software or automations mostly with visual tools and settings, adding small amounts of code only where needed.

    M

    Machine learning
    A branch of AI in which software learns patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules. It is used for predictions such as demand forecasting, lead scoring, or fraud detection.
    Make
    A visual automation platform (formerly called Integromat) for building multi-step workflows with branching, loops, and data transformation. Zapier vs. Make vs. n8n
    Missed-call text-back
    An automation that texts anyone whose call goes unanswered, so the conversation continues by message instead of the caller trying a competitor. Missed-call text-back guide
    Model Context Protocol (MCP)
    An open standard for connecting AI assistants and agents to tools and data sources, so one integration can be used by many AI applications. What is an AI agent?
    Multi-step workflow
    A workflow with several actions after its trigger, often across several apps, such as creating a contact, sending a message, and assigning a follow-up task.

    N

    n8n
    A source-available workflow automation tool that can be self-hosted or used in the cloud, popular with technical teams that want flexibility and control over their data. Zapier vs. Make vs. n8n
    Natural language processing (NLP)
    The field of AI concerned with understanding and generating human language, including classification, extraction, translation, and summarization.
    No-code
    Building apps or automations entirely with visual tools and settings, without writing code. How to automate repetitive tasks

    O

    OCR Optical Character Recognition
    Technology that turns images of text, such as scanned documents or photos of receipts, into machine-readable text. It is often paired with AI to pull out specific fields. How to automate data entry
    Orchestration
    Coordinating several automations, systems, or AI agents so they run in the right order and hand work to each other reliably.

    P

    Polling
    Checking an app on a schedule (for example, every five minutes) for new or changed data. Simpler to set up than webhooks, but slower to react.
    Power Automate
    Microsoft's workflow automation platform, closely integrated with Microsoft 365 apps such as Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and Excel. Automation platform picker
    Prompt
    The instructions and context you give an AI model. A clear prompt states the task, provides the information needed, specifies the output format, and sets rules. Lesson: writing prompts that work
    Prompt engineering
    The practice of designing and testing prompts so an AI model produces reliable, useful output for a specific task. AI prompt library

    R

    Rate limit
    A cap on how many requests an app's API accepts in a given time. Well-built automations slow down and retry when they hit one instead of failing.
    Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
    A technique that finds relevant passages in your own documents and gives them to an AI model along with the question, so answers are grounded in your information rather than the model's general knowledge. AI workflow automation
    Robotic process automation (RPA)
    Software bots that mimic a person clicking, typing, and copying in an application's screens. RPA is most useful when a system has no API or integration. What is RPA?

    S

    SaaS Software as a Service
    Software you use over the internet on a subscription, rather than installing and maintaining it yourself.
    Scheduled trigger
    A trigger that starts a workflow at set times, such as every weekday at 8 a.m. or on the first of each month, rather than in response to an event. How to automate reports
    Self-hosted
    Software that runs on servers you control rather than in the vendor's cloud. It offers more control over data in exchange for handling your own updates, security, and maintenance.
    Single source of truth
    One system that holds the authoritative version of a type of data, such as customer records, so every other tool and automation reads from it instead of keeping its own copy.
    SOP Standard operating procedure
    Written, step-by-step instructions for how a task is done. A good SOP is often the blueprint for an automation. Lesson: mapping a process
    Sync
    Keeping the same records matching in two or more apps, so a change in one is reflected in the others, either one way or both ways.

    T

    Token
    The unit AI models use to read and write text, roughly three quarters of an English word. AI providers usually charge per million tokens in and out. AI cost calculator
    Trigger
    The event that starts an automated workflow, such as a new form submission, an incoming email, a status change, or a scheduled time. Lesson: triggers, actions, and conditions

    V

    Vector database
    A database that stores embeddings so software can quickly find text with similar meaning. It is a common building block for retrieval-augmented generation.

    W

    Webhook
    An automatic message an app sends to a web address the moment something happens, so another system can react immediately instead of checking for changes. Lesson: connecting your tools
    Workflow
    A defined sequence of steps that turns an input into an outcome, such as a new inquiry becoming a booked appointment. Lesson: what automation is
    Workflow automation
    Using software to run the steps of a workflow automatically, triggered by events and following rules you define, so work moves between people and apps without manual effort. What is workflow automation?

    Z

    Zapier
    A popular no-code automation platform that connects thousands of apps through workflows called Zaps, each made of a trigger and one or more actions. Zapier vs. Make vs. n8n
