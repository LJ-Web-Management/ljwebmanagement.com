How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. As each piece of installed equipment approaches the end of its manufacturer or labor warranty, this automation automatically notifies both the customer and the sales team sixty and thirty days out, giving the customer a clear summary of what's expiring and an option to purchase an extended warranty or start planning for a replacement before something breaks uncovered.
2. The sales team receives a parallel alert with the customer's equipment age and service history so any outreach is informed rather than a cold pitch.
3. Customers who used to find out their warranty had lapsed only after an expensive repair bill now have time to make a decision in advance, and the sales team gets a steady, automatically generated list of warm replacement and extended-warranty opportunities instead of waiting for equipment to fail.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

Left unautomated, a warranty administrator spends hours on the parts of this job that don't require judgment: copying data, sending reminders, and tracking status by hand. Over a month, those small manual steps add up to a significant amount of lost time.

### With this automation

- Status updates triggered by real milestones in your systems
- Support requests routed to the right owner with full context
- Escalations when a response deadline is about to slip
- Fewer inbound status calls for your front desk
