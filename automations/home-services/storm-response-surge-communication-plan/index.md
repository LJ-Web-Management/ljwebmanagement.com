How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. During a major weather event, the automation automatically triggers a surge communication plan notifying every affected customer of expected delays and prioritizing emergency requests over routine scheduled visits, instead of the emergency dispatch lead trying to manually manage a flood of calls while the phone lines are already overwhelmed.
2. Customers get a proactive, honest heads-up about delays instead of being left wondering why nobody's answering the phone, and the office's limited staff spend the storm actually dispatching emergency crews instead of fielding the same "when will someone come" call over and over.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

Done manually, an emergency dispatch lead has to remember to follow up on every case individually, often relying on memory, phone calls, and whatever notes didn't get lost. None of it is difficult, it's just slow, repetitive, and easy to fall behind on.

### With this automation

- Status updates triggered by real milestones in your systems
- Support requests routed to the right owner with full context
- Escalations when a response deadline is about to slip
- Fewer inbound status calls for your front desk
