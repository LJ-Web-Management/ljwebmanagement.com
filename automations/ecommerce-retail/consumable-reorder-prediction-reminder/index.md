How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. Based on the typical reorder timing for a consumable product, customers automatically receive a "running low, time to reorder" reminder timed to when they're actually likely running out, rather than a generic reminder sent on an arbitrary fixed schedule that either arrives too early or too late.
2. The subscription manager can see reorder rates climb once reminders are timed to real consumption patterns instead of a flat calendar interval, and customers appreciate a reminder that shows up exactly when they were about to need it rather than nagging them before they've used half the product.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

The manual approach means a subscription manager handles each case reactively, after someone has already noticed something went wrong. It rarely feels urgent in the moment, but it adds up fast.

### With this automation

- Win-back sequences triggered by inactivity, not guesswork
- Renewals and memberships that never lapse silently
- Referral and loyalty programs that track themselves
- Segmented campaigns built from actual purchase history
