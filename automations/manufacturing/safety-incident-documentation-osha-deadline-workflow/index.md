How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. When a worker reports a safety incident through a simple mobile form (near miss, injury, or property damage) the safety coordinator and plant manager are notified within minutes with the location, description, and any photos attached.
2. The report automatically opens the required documentation packet, incident details, witness statements, and corrective action log, so nothing has to be assembled from scratch after the fact.
3. Because OSHA recordkeeping and reporting deadlines vary by severity (a recordable injury has different timelines than a fatality or hospitalization), the system reads the severity flagged on the intake form and sets the correct follow-up reminders automatically, at 24 hours, 5 days, and whatever window applies.
4. What used to depend on someone remembering the exact regulatory deadline now runs on its own clock, and no incident sits undocumented past the window that matters.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

The manual version of this puts the entire workload on a safety coordinator, who has to juggle phone calls, emails, and spreadsheets just to keep things moving. Over a month, those small manual steps add up to a significant amount of lost time.

### With this automation
