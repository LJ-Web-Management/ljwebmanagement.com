How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. Orders placed through DoorDash, UberEats, and Grubhub are automatically synced directly into the POS and kitchen display system the instant they come in, instead of a runner staring at three separate tablets stacked at the end of the pass trying to catch every order across every platform during a rush.
2. Every delivery order shows up in the same queue as dine-in and pickup tickets, prioritized by the same rules, so the kitchen fires food in the order it's actually needed rather than whichever tablet happened to buzz loudest, and missed or delayed orders across all three platforms drop sharply once nothing depends on a human noticing a screen.

Tools it connects

- DoorDash, UberEats, and Grubhub merchant portals
- Zapier or Make.com
- POS system (e.g., Toast or Square)

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

The manual version of this puts the entire workload on a delivery operations manager, who has to juggle phone calls, emails, and spreadsheets just to keep things moving. It rarely feels urgent in the moment, but it adds up fast.

### With this automation

- Low-stock and reorder alerts before you run out
- Vendor and subcontractor confirmations that chase themselves
- Preventive maintenance scheduled by usage or date
- Work orders and project milestones that update stakeholders
