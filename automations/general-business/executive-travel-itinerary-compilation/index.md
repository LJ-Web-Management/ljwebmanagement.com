How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. When a flight, hotel, or car service is booked for an executive trip, the confirmation emails land automatically in a dedicated inbox that the workflow monitors around the clock, pulling out the relevant times, addresses, and confirmation numbers instead of the travel and events coordinator copying details by hand into a document.
2. Everything gets compiled into a single, chronological itinerary in Google Docs, complete with time zone conversions and a map link for each stop, and the finished itinerary is texted to the executive through Twilio the morning of departure along with a calendar invite for every leg of the trip.
3. Any flight delay or gate change picked up from the airline's notification triggers an instant Slack alert to the coordinator so a car service or meeting can be adjusted before the executive even lands.
4. Trip planning that used to take an hour of manual assembly now finishes itself.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

The manual approach means a travel and events coordinator handles each case reactively, after someone has already noticed something went wrong. It rarely feels urgent in the moment, but it adds up fast.

### With this automation

- Low-stock and reorder alerts before you run out
- Vendor and subcontractor confirmations that chase themselves
- Preventive maintenance scheduled by usage or date
- Work orders and project milestones that update stakeholders
