    How it works

    What this automation does, step by step

    1. A simple inventory count of recurring office supplies, paper, coffee, cleaning products, printer toner, lives in a Google Sheet that staff update with a quick form whenever they notice something running low.

    2. The moment any item's quantity drops below its preset reorder threshold, the workflow automatically generates a purchase order and either emails it to the approved vendor or places the order directly through the vendor's punch-out catalog if one is connected, then notifies the office administrator in Slack that the order went out along with the expected delivery date.

    3. High-cost items over a set dollar amount route for a quick approval in Slack before the order fires instead of placing automatically.

    4. The break room and supply closet stop running empty at the worst possible moment, and nobody has to remember to check stock levels manually.

    The manual way vs. the automated way

    Without automation

    Without this system, an office administrator manually pieces together information from different places, then has to relay it to everyone who needs it. It rarely feels urgent in the moment, but it adds up fast.

    With this automation

    • Low-stock and reorder alerts before you run out
    • Vendor and subcontractor confirmations that chase themselves
    • Preventive maintenance scheduled by usage or date
    • Work orders and project milestones that update stakeholders
