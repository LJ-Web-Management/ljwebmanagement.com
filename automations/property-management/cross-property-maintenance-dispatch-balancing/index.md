How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. For companies with maintenance staff serving multiple properties, the automation balances and routes work orders based on each technician's current location and workload, instead of the maintenance coordinator dispatching by gut feel and accidentally sending the same tech across town twice in one afternoon while another sits idle nearby.
2. Jobs get assigned to whichever available technician can actually reach the unit fastest, and average response time across the whole portfolio improves noticeably once dispatch decisions are based on real-time location and workload data instead of a fixed rotation that ignores where everyone actually is.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

Without automation, a maintenance coordinator has to notice problems after they've already happened, then scramble to fix them manually one at a time. None of it is difficult, it's just slow, repetitive, and easy to fall behind on.

### With this automation

- Low-stock and reorder alerts before you run out
- Vendor and subcontractor confirmations that chase themselves
- Preventive maintenance scheduled by usage or date
- Work orders and project milestones that update stakeholders
