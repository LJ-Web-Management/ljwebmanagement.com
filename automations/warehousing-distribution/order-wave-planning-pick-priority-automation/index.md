How it works

## What this automation does, step by step

1. Two hours before each pick wave is released, this automation analyzes open orders by carrier cutoff time, order priority, pick zone, and available labor, and builds an optimized wave plan that maximizes throughput and ensures on-time carrier departures.
2. The wave plan is automatically pushed to the WMS and pick task assignments are distributed to available pickers by zone, no supervisor manually building the plan or radio-ing assignments across the floor.
3. If an order is at risk of missing its carrier cutoff due to a staffing gap or pick exception, the DC manager receives an alert with enough lead time to reassign resources or expedite.

## The manual way vs. the automated way

### Without automation

The manual approach means a distribution center manager handles each case reactively, after someone has already noticed something went wrong. It rarely feels urgent in the moment, but it adds up fast.

### With this automation

- Low-stock and reorder alerts before you run out
- Vendor and subcontractor confirmations that chase themselves
- Preventive maintenance scheduled by usage or date
- Work orders and project milestones that update stakeholders
