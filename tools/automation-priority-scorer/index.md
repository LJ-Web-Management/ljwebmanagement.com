Volume

How often it happens. 5 means many times a day.

Time

Minutes per run, including switching apps. 5 means 15 minutes or more.

Cost of a miss

What a slip costs. 5 means a lost customer or a late payment.

Rule clarity

Could a new hire follow written steps? 5 means clear if-this-then-that rules.

Stability

How settled the process is. 5 means unchanged for a year.

## How to use the scorer

1. **List your candidates.** Spend ten minutes with your team writing down every task that is repetitive, rule-based, or annoying. Most businesses find 15 to 30.
2. **Score each factor from 1 to 5.** Be quick and honest; relative scores matter more than precision.
3. **Read the shortlist.** Tasks scoring 18 or more out of 25 are strong first candidates. When two tie, pick the one closer to revenue.

## Why these five factors

The best first automations happen often (volume), take real effort (time), hurt when they slip (cost of a miss), follow clear rules (rule clarity), and will not change next month (stability). A task that scores high on all five pays back fast and rarely breaks. A task with low rule clarity or stability is a better candidate for process cleanup first, or for an AI-assisted step with human review.

## Frequently asked questions

**What should a small business automate first?**

Usually a high-volume, rule-based task close to revenue: replying to new leads, appointment reminders, invoicing and payment reminders, or client intake. They score high on volume, cost of a miss, and rule clarity.

**What if a task scores high but the process is messy?**

Clean up the process first. Write the steps down, remove the ones that exist only because of old tools, and agree on how exceptions are handled. Automating a messy process just makes the mess happen faster.

**Where is my list saved?**

Only in this browser, using local storage. Nothing is sent to us. Clearing your browser data or using a private window will reset it.
