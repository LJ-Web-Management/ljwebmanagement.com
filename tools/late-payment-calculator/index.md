# How much cash is stuck in unpaid invoices?

> See how much cash is tied up in slow-paying invoices, how much faster collection would free up, and what chasing payments costs your team each year.

Slow payments do not just delay revenue; they lock up cash you could be using. See how much faster invoicing and automated reminders could free up.

## How the calculation works

At any moment, the money customers owe you is roughly your **daily revenue × the average days it takes to get paid**. Daily revenue is monthly revenue × 12 &divide; 365. Cut the days and the same revenue ties up less cash.

- **Cash freed up** = daily revenue × (days today &minus; target days).
- **Borrowing cost avoided** = cash freed × your interest rate, per year.
- **Chasing cost** = hours per week × 52 × hourly cost.

## Where the days go

Most late payments are slow for boring reasons: the invoice went out a week after the work, it lacked a purchase order number, it landed in the wrong inbox, or paying required a check. Automation removes those delays:

- Create and send the invoice the moment the job closes or the order ships
- Include a pay-online link and the details the customer's accounts team needs
- Send polite reminders before the due date, on it, and after it, on a schedule
- Retry failed card payments and ask customers to update expired cards
- Alert a person only when an account needs a real conversation

## Frequently asked questions

**What is DSO?**

Days sales outstanding is the average number of days it takes to collect payment after a sale. A simple estimate is accounts receivable &divide; revenue for the period × the number of days in the period.

**What is a good number of days to get paid?**

Close to your stated payment terms. If your terms are net 30 and you average 45 days, the 15-day gap is mostly process friction that reminders and easier payment can close.

**Will automatic payment reminders upset customers?**

Not when they are polite, accurate, and well timed. Most customers appreciate a reminder before the due date with a direct payment link. Keep the tone friendly and stop reminders the moment a payment arrives.

**Can automation handle partial payments and disputes?**

It can detect them and route them to a person, which is usually the right approach. Automation handles the routine majority; people handle the conversations that need judgment.
