Document workflow automation uses software to handle the life of a document: requesting and collecting it, generating it from a template, routing it for review and approval, getting it signed, and filing and tracking it, including reminders before it expires. It replaces emailed attachments, chasing, and copying details into Word files by hand.

Documents are behind many of the slowest processes in a business: onboarding, contracts, compliance, and hiring. This guide breaks document automation into five stages you can tackle one at a time.

Stage 1: Collect

Chasing clients and staff for documents is one of the most common time sinks we see. Automate it:

  • Send a secure upload link or portal with a checklist of exactly what is needed.
  • Track what has been received against the checklist automatically.
  • Send friendly reminders for anything missing at set intervals, and stop when it arrives.
  • Rename and file each upload consistently, such as ClientName_DocumentType_Date.
  • Use AI to check that an upload is the right type of document and is readable, and to extract key details.

Stage 2: Generate

Instead of opening last month's proposal and editing it (and occasionally leaving the old client's name in), generate documents from templates:

  1. Create a template with placeholders such as {client_name}, {service}, and {price}.
  2. Add conditional sections that appear only for certain services, locations, or client types.
  3. Trigger generation from an event: a deal reaches "proposal," an employee is hired, a job is completed.
  4. Save the result as a PDF in the right folder and attach it to the CRM record.

Common candidates: proposals, quotes, contracts, engagement letters, offer letters, certificates, and reports.

Stage 3: Review and approve

Route documents to the right reviewer based on rules (value, department, document type), with reminders when approvals stall and a record of who approved what and when. AI can help reviewers by summarizing long documents and flagging clauses that differ from your standard template, but the approval decision stays with a person.

Stage 4: Sign

E-signature tools turn signing into a few clicks. Automate around them:

  • Send for signature automatically when a document is approved
  • Remind signers who have not completed it
  • When fully signed, file the signed copy, update the CRM, and trigger the next step (onboarding, invoicing, scheduling)
  • Notify the team that the deal or hire is official

Stage 5: File and track

  • Consistent storage: one folder structure, one naming convention, linked from the customer or employee record.
  • Expiry tracking: licenses, certifications, insurance certificates, contracts, and permits each have an expiry date and an automated reminder schedule.
  • Retention: flag documents for review or deletion according to your retention policy.
  • Access control: limit who can see sensitive documents, and keep an audit trail.
Sensitive documents need extra care

Identity documents, medical records, and financial statements should be collected through secure links rather than email, stored with access controls, and only shared with AI services covered by appropriate agreements. Check the regulations that apply to your industry.
