In a small business, onboarding usually lives in one person's head. When that person is busy, a new hire shows up on day one without a laptop, logins, or a clear plan, and spends their first week waiting. It is a poor first impression and it slows down the moment they can start contributing.

A simple automated checklist fixes most of this. You do not need an HR department, just a reliable trigger and a list of who needs to do what by when.

Start from one trigger

Everything should kick off from a single event, usually the signed offer letter. When the signature lands, the automation reads the new hire's role, manager, location, and start date, and builds the rest of the plan from those fields.

The pre-day-one checklist

Paperwork (sent automatically, tracked until complete)

  • Tax withholding forms (federal W-4 and any state equivalent)
  • Form I-9 Section 1, which the employee must complete by their first day of work
  • Direct deposit and emergency contact details
  • Handbook and policy acknowledgments
  • Any role-specific agreements or certifications
Deadlines that are not optional

Employers must complete Form I-9 Section 2 within three business days of the start date, and federal law requires reporting new hires to your state within 20 days (some states require it sooner). Build these deadlines into reminders for whoever owns them.

Accounts and equipment (assigned to the right owners)

  • Email, chat, and core software accounts created, based on role
  • Laptop, phone, uniform, keys, or badge ordered and assigned
  • Payroll and benefits enrollment started with your provider
  • Calendar invites for orientation and first-week meetings

Each task goes to its owner (IT, the manager, payroll) with a due date calculated from the start date, and reminders if it is not marked done.

Welcome (sent to the new hire)

  • A welcome message from their manager a few days before they start
  • What to expect on day one: time, place, parking, dress code, who to ask for
  • A first-week schedule

Keep certifications from lapsing

For teams with licenses or certifications (technicians, clinicians, drivers, food handlers, advisors), track expiration dates in one place and send reminders at 90, 60, and 30 days, escalating to the manager if a renewal is not confirmed. This is one of the most valuable HR automations in regulated industries, because a lapsed credential can take someone off the schedule.

Automate offboarding, too

When someone leaves, the same structure runs in reverse:

  • Access removed from email, software, and buildings on the last day
  • Equipment return requested and tracked
  • Final pay and benefits handled according to your state's rules
  • Exit survey sent
  • Files and accounts transferred to a named owner

Other HR workflows worth automating

  • Interview scheduling with self-booking links and automatic reminders
  • Time-off requests routed to the right manager and reflected on the team calendar
  • Timesheet reminders and exception alerts before payroll runs
  • Review cycles with reminders for self-reviews and manager reviews
