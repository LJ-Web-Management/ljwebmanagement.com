You can automate WhatsApp in two ways: the free WhatsApp Business app offers greeting messages, away messages, and quick replies, while the WhatsApp Business Platform (the API, usually through a provider) lets software send reminders and notifications, run chatbots, and connect WhatsApp to your CRM or booking system. Business-initiated messages need customer opt-in and pre-approved templates.

In many countries, WhatsApp is where customers already talk to businesses. This guide explains what you can automate, which option you need, and how to stay within the rules. WhatsApp's features and policies change regularly, so confirm the details with WhatsApp's current business documentation before you build.

Option 1: The WhatsApp Business app

The free app is ideal for small businesses handling conversations by hand, with a few helpful automations:

  • Greeting message: an automatic welcome when a new customer messages you, or after a period of no contact.
  • Away message: an automatic reply outside your business hours, with when you will respond and a link to book.
  • Quick replies: saved answers to common questions, inserted with a shortcut.
  • Labels: organize chats by stage, such as new lead, booked, or paid.
  • Catalog: show products or services customers can browse in the chat.

The app does not connect to your other systems, so it cannot send reminders when an appointment is booked in your scheduler, for example.

Option 2: The WhatsApp Business Platform (API)

For automation connected to your other tools, you need the Business Platform. Most small businesses access it through a provider (a messaging platform, CRM, or customer service tool with WhatsApp built in) rather than building on it directly. With it, you can:

Booking confirmations and remindersAppointment booked in your scheduler

The rules to know

  • Opt-in: customers must agree to receive WhatsApp messages from your business before you message them first.
  • Templates: messages your business starts, such as reminders and updates, generally use templates that WhatsApp has reviewed and approved.
  • Conversation window: after a customer messages you, you can reply freely for a limited period; after that, you need a template again.
  • Pricing: the Platform charges by message type and country, and providers may add fees.
  • Quality: if many people block or report your messages, your sending ability can be limited. Only send messages people expect and value.

Designing a WhatsApp chatbot

  • Start with a menu of your top five reasons people message you
  • Answer each from approved information, with links where useful
  • Collect key details (name, service, preferred time) before handing off
  • Offer "talk to a person" at every step, and route it to your team inbox
  • If you add AI, ground it in your own content and tell it to hand off when unsure

WhatsApp, SMS, or email?

Use the channel your customers prefer. In regions where WhatsApp dominates, it often gets faster responses than email. In the United States, SMS is still the default for reminders and quick replies. Many businesses use both: a text for the reminder, WhatsApp for customers who start conversations there. The same automation logic applies to each; see missed-call text-back and appointment reminders for message timing and templates.

Where to start

If you are on the free app, turn on greeting and away messages and create quick replies for your ten most common questions today. When you are ready to connect WhatsApp to bookings or your CRM, choose a provider that supports the Business Platform and start with confirmations and reminders.
