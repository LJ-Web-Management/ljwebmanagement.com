There are dozens of automation tools, and the marketing makes them all sound perfect. The truth is that most small businesses can succeed with any of the major platforms. What matters is choosing the one that fits your team, your apps, and your volume, and then building well.

First, check what you already have

Before adding a platform, look inside the apps you use. CRMs, scheduling tools, email marketing platforms, accounting software, and help desks all include built-in automations: reminders, follow-up sequences, assignment rules, recurring invoices. They are usually included in your plan and are very reliable because the vendor maintains them. Use them for anything that stays within one app.

You need an automation platform when a workflow crosses between apps.

Five questions that decide it

  1. Who will build and maintain it? If it is a busy owner, ease of use wins. If you have a technical person, flexibility wins.
  2. Which apps are involved? Check that each platform supports your apps and the specific triggers and actions you need, not just the app name.
  3. How much volume? Estimate runs per month and steps per run. Platforms count usage differently (tasks, operations, executions), so the same workflow can cost very different amounts.
  4. How sensitive is the data? Health, financial, and legal data may require specific agreements, data residency, or self-hosting. Check each vendor's compliance documentation.
  5. How complex is the logic? Simple "when this, do that" suits anything. Loops, heavy data transformation, and AI steps favor Make, n8n, or code.
Check prices yourself

Automation platforms change their plans and pricing often. Use each vendor's current pricing page with your volume estimate, and remember to include the cost of the apps' own plans, since some integrations require a higher tier.

Avoid these traps

  • Tool sprawl: workflows spread across three platforms that nobody fully understands. Pick one main platform where possible.
  • Personal accounts: automations built on an employee's personal login stop working when they leave. Use a shared business account with admin access.
  • No documentation: name every workflow clearly and keep a short description of what it does, its trigger, and its owner.
  • Choosing by brand, not fit: the best-known tool is not always the best fit for your volume or data.

Build it yourself or get help?

Building a few simple workflows yourself is a great way to learn. Bring in help when a workflow touches money or customers at scale, needs custom logic or AI, or when the hours you would spend learning are better spent running your business. A good partner will build on whichever platform fits, document everything, and leave you in control of your accounts.
