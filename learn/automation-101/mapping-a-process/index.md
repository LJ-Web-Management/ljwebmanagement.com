# Mapping a process before you automate it

> How to write down a process step by step, simplify it, and turn it into a clear blueprint for an automation, with a simple template you can copy.

How to write down a process step by step, simplify it, and turn it into a clear blueprint for an automation, with a simple template you can copy.

8 min lesson Beginner Free, no sign-up

In this lesson

- Automating a messy process makes the mess happen faster. Map and simplify first.
- Write each step as one action, noting who does it, in which app, and what triggers it.
- Mark every decision point and every exception, and decide who owns them.
- A good process map is the blueprint for the automation and the SOP for your team.

The most common reason automations disappoint is not the technology. It is automating a process nobody had written down. Hidden steps get missed, exceptions break things, and the team ends up working around the system. Ten minutes of mapping prevents most of that.

## Step 1: Name the start and the finish

Every process turns an input into an outcome. Write both down in one line:

- _"A new web inquiry becomes a booked consultation."_
- _"A completed job becomes a paid invoice."_
- _"A signed client becomes a fully onboarded account."_

The start is usually your trigger. The finish is how you will know the automation worked.

## Step 2: List every step as it happens today

Walk through the process with the person who actually does it. Write one action per line, starting with a verb, and note three things for each step:

| # | Step | Who | Where (app) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Read the new inquiry email | Office manager | Gmail |
| 2 | Copy name, phone, and service into the CRM | Office manager | CRM |
| 3 | Reply with available times | Office manager | Gmail |
| 4 | If no reply in 2 days, follow up | Office manager (if remembered) | Gmail |
| 5 | Book the time in the calendar | Office manager | Calendar |
| 6 | Send a confirmation with directions | Office manager | Gmail |

Record what really happens, including the workarounds, not what the process is supposed to be. "If remembered" is exactly the kind of note that shows where automation will help.

## Step 3: Mark decisions and exceptions

Highlight every point where someone makes a choice, and every situation that does not fit the normal path:

- **Decisions:** Is this a new or existing customer? Is it commercial or residential? Is the amount over the approval limit?
- **Exceptions:** The inquiry is spam. The phone number is missing. The customer asks for a time outside business hours.

For each one, decide: can a clear rule handle it, does it need AI to interpret something, or should it go to a person? Then name the person.

## Step 4: Simplify before you automate

Now look at the map with fresh eyes and ask of every step:

- Does this step add value, or does it exist only because of an old tool or habit?
- Is anyone entering the same information twice?
- Could two steps be combined, or done at the same time?
- Is there an approval nobody actually needs?
- Can the customer do this step themselves, such as picking a time from a booking link?

In the example above, a self-service booking link removes steps 3 to 5 entirely. The automation that remains is smaller and more reliable.

## Step 5: Write the target process

Rewrite the map as it should run, marking which steps will be automated:

1. **Trigger:** a new inquiry arrives through the website form.
2. **Automated:** create or update the contact in the CRM.
3. **Automated:** text and email a reply within a minute with a booking link.
4. **Automated:** if not booked in 2 days, send a follow-up; repeat once more on day 5.
5. **Automated:** when booked, send a confirmation with directions and add reminders.
6. **Person:** handle any inquiry flagged as unusual or high value.

This document is now two things at once: the blueprint for whoever builds the automation, and the standard operating procedure (SOP) for your team. Keep it somewhere everyone can find it, and update it when the process changes.

**Tip: let AI help you write it**

Describe the process out loud, transcribe it, and ask an AI assistant to turn it into numbered steps with who, where, and decision points. The [prompt library](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/ai-prompt-library/) has a ready-made prompt for this. Always check the result with the person who does the work.
