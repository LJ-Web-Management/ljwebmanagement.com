# Spotting tasks worth automating

> How to find the repetitive work hiding in your week, and how to score each task so you automate the ones that pay back fastest.

How to find the repetitive work hiding in your week, and how to score each task so you automate the ones that pay back fastest.

8 min lesson Beginner Free, no sign-up

In this lesson

- The best candidates are frequent, rule-based, and painful when they slip.
- Look for copy-and-paste between apps, reminders people have to remember, and messages you type again and again.
- Score each task on volume, time, cost of a miss, rule clarity, and stability.
- Start with one task close to revenue, not the most impressive one.

Every business has a hidden layer of work: the copying, checking, reminding, and retyping that never appears on a job description but eats hours every week. The first skill in automation is seeing that work clearly. The second is choosing which piece to automate first.

## Where repetitive work hides

Spend a week noticing these patterns. Each one is a strong automation signal:

- **Copy and paste between apps.** Typing details from a form into the CRM, from the CRM into an invoice, from an invoice into a spreadsheet.
- **Things people have to remember.** Follow up with that lead on Thursday. Remind the client about their appointment. Chase the unpaid invoice.
- **Messages you type again and again.** Booking confirmations, "here is what to expect," payment reminders, review requests.
- **Status checking.** Logging into a portal to see whether an order shipped, a document was signed, or a payment arrived.
- **Reports built by hand.** Exporting data every Monday and rebuilding the same spreadsheet.
- **Handoffs.** Sales closes a deal and has to tell operations, who has to tell the project lead, who has to email the client.

**Try this**

Ask everyone on your team: "What did you do this week that a computer should have done?" Write every answer down without judging it. Most teams list 15 to 30 items in ten minutes.

## The five-factor score

Once you have a list, score each item from 1 (low) to 5 (high) on five factors. Add them up for a total out of 25.

| Factor | Score high when... |
| --- | --- |
| **Volume** | It happens many times a week: every lead, every booking, every invoice. |
| **Time** | Each occurrence takes several minutes, including switching between apps. |
| **Cost of a miss** | When it slips you lose money or trust: a cold lead, a late payment, a missed deadline. |
| **Rule clarity** | You could explain the steps to a new hire as "if this, then that." |
| **Stability** | The process has not changed much in a year and is not about to. |

Anything scoring 18 or more is a strong first candidate. The free [automation priority scorer](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/automation-priority-scorer/) does the math and ranks your list for you.

## Reading the scores

The total matters, but so does the shape:

- **High volume, high cost of a miss, clear rules:** automate first. Lead follow-up, appointment reminders, invoicing, and intake usually land here.
- **High volume, unclear rules:** the process needs cleaning up first (lesson three), or an AI step with a person reviewing, such as sorting free-text emails.
- **Low volume, high time:** worth automating later, often with a template or checklist before full automation. A quarterly report is a typical example.
- **Low stability:** wait. If the process is about to change, automating it now means rebuilding it soon.

## The shortcut question

If you only have time for one question, ask: **"What happens when this task is forgotten?"** When the honest answer is "we lose a customer" or "we do not get paid," the task belongs at the top of your list, whatever else it scores.

## Common first wins

Across hundreds of small businesses, the same workflows tend to win first because they score high on volume, cost of a miss, and rule clarity:

- Instant replies and follow-up for new leads, including [texting back missed calls](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/missed-call-text-back/)
- Booking confirmations and [appointment reminders](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/reduce-no-shows-appointment-reminders/)
- [Invoices and payment reminders](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/automate-invoicing-payment-reminders/) sent without anyone remembering
- [Client intake and onboarding](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/client-onboarding-automation/) forms, documents, and welcome messages
- [Review requests](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/automate-review-requests/) after a completed job or visit

Pick one. In the next lesson, you will map it step by step so it is ready to automate.
