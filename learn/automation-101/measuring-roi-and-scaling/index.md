# Measuring results and scaling what works

> How to measure an automation's time savings and revenue impact, calculate payback and ROI, and build a roadmap for what to automate next.

How to measure an automation's time savings and revenue impact, calculate payback and ROI, and build a roadmap for what to automate next.

8 min lesson Beginner Free, no sign-up

In this lesson

- Measure a baseline before launch so you can prove the change.
- Count both time saved and revenue protected: faster replies, fewer no-shows, quicker payments.
- Payback period is build cost divided by monthly net benefit.
- Scale by reusing what works: one workflow end to end, then the next highest-scoring one.

Automation should earn its place. This final lesson shows how to prove an automation is working, put a number on what it returns, and use that evidence to decide what to automate next.

## Measure before you launch

Pick one or two numbers the automation should move, and record them for a few weeks before launch:

| Automation | Baseline to record |
| --- | --- |
| Lead follow-up | Average time to first reply, share of leads followed up, close rate |
| Appointment reminders | No-show rate, staff time spent on reminder calls |
| Invoicing and reminders | Days from job completion to invoice, average days to get paid |
| Data entry | Hours per week spent on entry, error rate |
| Reporting | Hours to build the weekly report, how often it is late |

If you skip this, you will be left with "it feels faster," which is hard to build on.

## Count both kinds of value

- **Time saved:** hours removed per month multiplied by the fully loaded hourly cost of whoever did the work. The [time savings calculator](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/time-savings-calculator/) does this.
- **Revenue protected or gained:** more leads converted, fewer missed appointments, faster payments, fewer errors. These are often worth more than the hours. Try the [lead response](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/lead-response-calculator/), [no-show](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/no-show-cost-calculator/), and [late payment](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/late-payment-calculator/) calculators.

## Payback and ROI

Two simple formulas cover most decisions:

- **Monthly net benefit** = value of time saved + revenue gained &minus; running costs.
- **Payback period** = build cost &divide; monthly net benefit.

An automation that costs $2,000 to build and nets $500 a month pays for itself in four months. Everything after that is return. For a fuller view over several years, use the [automation payback calculator](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/tools/automation-payback-calculator/) or read the [automation ROI guide](https://www.ljwebmanagement.com/guides/automation-roi/).

**Be conservative**

Use cautious estimates, and run the numbers with revenue gains at zero first. If the automation pays back on time savings alone, the revenue upside is a bonus. If it only works with optimistic revenue numbers, look for a simpler version.

## Review after 30 and 90 days

Compare your numbers against the baseline and ask:

- Did the target number move as expected?
- How many errors or exceptions needed a person, and why?
- Is the team actually using the time it got back?
- What did customers notice, good or bad?
- What small change would make it better?

## Build your automation roadmap

With one success behind you, return to your scored task list from lesson two. Re-score it with what you have learned, then work down the list one workflow at a time. Look for reuse: the CRM cleanup you did for lead follow-up makes onboarding easier; the invoice trigger you built makes review requests trivial.

Over time, the individual workflows join up into a system: inquiries become customers, jobs become invoices, invoices become payments and reviews, with people focused on the work only they can do.
